The training

Does it cost anything?

No. Our trainings are free for the people taking them, and we bring the equipment.

Do I get certified?

Yes. Everyone who completes the course gets a Stop the Bleed Coalition course completion certificate by email. Nobody has failed one of our classes yet.

How long does a class take?

Usually about an hour, including the hands-on practice.

Do I need any medical background?

None. Most of our trainees have never done this before. The class is built for people who aren't doctors, nurses or EMTs.

Can you teach the class in Spanish?

Yes. Just tell us in your email.

How big or small can our group be?

We've taught classes of a few people and classes of a full lecture room. Tell us roughly how many you expect and we'll plan instructors and equipment around it.

What do we need to provide?

A room with tables and the people. If there's a projector we'll use it, but we can teach without one.

Is this the same as CPR or first aid?

No. CPR is for when someone's heart stops. STOP THE BLEED® is for severe bleeding, which can kill someone before their heart ever stops. They work well together, and lots of our trainees have taken CPR too.

Can I help teach?

Some of our trainees have come back to assist and later teach classes themselves. If you're interested, take a class first, then email us.

Why it matters

How common is death from bleeding?

Uncontrolled bleeding is the number one cause of preventable death after an injury, and a person can die from blood loss in as little as five minutes. That's often less time than it takes for an ambulance to arrive, which is why bystanders matter.

Sources: STOP THE BLEED®, Penn State Health.

Where are the bleeding control kits at UTSA?

UTSA's bleeding control kits are mounted with the AEDs in campus buildings. Most people have never noticed them: in our surveys, 73% of trainees weren't at all familiar with where the kits on campus are.

We're working on a way to fix that. Our campus kit finder is an early prototype built from UTSA PD's kit list. It isn't affiliated with or approved by UTSA, and the locations are approximate, so don't rely on it in an emergency. In an emergency, call 911.

Is the survey data on the home page from a research study?
